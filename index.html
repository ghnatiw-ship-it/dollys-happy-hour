Late-night happy hour: when it pays, and when it doesn't

Andy's proposal cuts five items by 20–50% from 10 PM on Wednesdays and Thursdays. Costs come from the Dolly's beverage app and sales from the POS. This is the business case: what each price needs to sell to stay as profitable as today, how much guests switching from full-price drinks can cost, and the numbers that tell us to keep it or drop it after a trial.

The cocktails cleared their bar

02 · THE MECHANICS

Every dollar off comes out of profit

Product cost stays the same when the price drops, so the whole discount comes off the profit on that drink. To stand still we need enough extra drinks to make it back.

Sales needed = profit per item at regular price ÷ profit per item at happy hour price

Our drinks run 78–84% margin, which is why the cuts are affordable at all. The Blonde keeps $6.05 of its $8.05 profit at $8. The Margarita keeps only $4.41 of $12.41 at $8.

03 · THRESHOLD BY ITEM

How many times today's sales each price needs

A bar at 2× means we must sell double what we sell late on a Wed/Thu today just to make the same profit. Anything above that is gain.

Green under 1.5× is easy, amber up to 2.2× is workable, red needs a real crowd. Pizza has no recipe cost in the Dolly's food cost app yet, so it uses the app's 32% target ($7.23 on a $22.60 pizza); at Andy's $2.25 it would need 2.2×.

04 · IN DRINKS, NOT MULTIPLES

Big multiples, small numbers

Late on a Wed or Thu we sell about 9 Blondes, 2 Margaritas, 1 Espresso Martini, less than half a glass of house wine and almost no whole pizza (late food is mostly $5–6 slices). So even the red items only need a few extra a night.

Grey = sold per night now (average of 19 Wed/Thu nights, Aug 19 – Oct 21, Auphan POS). Amber = needed per night to make the same profit at the happy hour price.

05 · THE PRICE LADDER

Each dollar off costs more than the last

Extra items needed per night as the price steps down. The bars grow faster at each step, which is why the last dollar off is the one to question.

06 · THE MAIN RISK

Guests switching from full-price drinks

The discount on the few happy hour items we already sell late only gives away about $46 a night. The bigger cost is a guest who would have paid full price and orders the cheap drink instead.

From 2,956 other drinks sold after 10 PM over 19 nights, costed in the beverage app: about 70 beers a night make $5.83 each (32% cost) and about 83 shots and highballs make $8.38 each (18% cost). A beer drinker moving to an $8 Blonde costs us nothing. A shot drinker moving to an $8 Margarita costs $3.97. Labels show the new happy hour drinks needed to cover one swap.

10 · RECOMMENDATION

What we're proposing

  1. Program the full happy hour in Auphan for both nights. Last week Wednesday rang nothing and Thursday rang only the cocktails. We can't judge the trial until the Blonde, wine and pizza prices ring on both nights.
  2. Dolly's Blonde at $8. It needs a third more pints, about 3 a night, and it's already the best late seller. A guest switching from another beer costs us nothing.
  3. Keep cocktails at $10, as they rang last Thursday. The Margarita's break-even drops from 2.8× to 1.9× and the Espresso Martini's from 2.35× to 1.76×; a shot drinker switching costs half as much. If $8 is the headline, keep it to Wed/Thu only and hold it to the scorecard.
  4. House wine at $8 is fine. It needs 1.8×, but we only sell about half a glass late now, so it's less than one extra glass a night.
  5. Cost the pizzas in the food cost app. None of the 7 pizzas has a recipe cost yet. At the 32% target ($7.23) the $11.50 pizza needs 3.6×; at Andy's $2.25 it needs 2.2×. It also competes with the $5–6 slices that make up most late food.
  6. Count added costs in the decision. If the window needs an extra bartender, DJ or paid promotion, every $50 a night raises the target by about 9 items.
